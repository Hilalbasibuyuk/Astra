# Observatory Weather and Observation Conditions

## Purpose

This document describes environmental conditions relevant to astronomical observations. It supports ASTRA's assessment of weather telemetry and the identification of conditions that may reduce observation quality or create operational risks.

## Cloud Cover

Cloud cover can reduce the amount of light reaching the telescope and can make observing conditions unstable.

An increase in measured cloud cover may indicate:

- Reduced transparency.
- Intermittent obstruction of the target.
- Deteriorating observation conditions.
- A need to reassess the current observation plan.

Cloud-cover sensors and weather services may have different measurement methods and limitations. Their readings should be interpreted in the context of the observatory's own instruments and procedures.

## Atmospheric Seeing

Seeing describes the apparent blurring or motion of astronomical sources caused by atmospheric turbulence. Poor seeing can reduce image sharpness and affect measurements that require precise angular resolution.

Seeing may deteriorate because of atmospheric turbulence, local thermal effects, or changing environmental conditions.

A poor image-quality measurement does not automatically imply poor seeing. Focus, tracking, optics, and camera configuration should also be considered.

## Wind Speed

Wind can affect telescope pointing stability, dome operation, and the safety of observatory equipment.

A sudden increase in wind speed should be evaluated against:

- The telescope and dome manufacturer's limits.
- Local observatory safety procedures.
- The current telescope and dome state.
- The rate and duration of the wind-speed change.

ASTRA must not invent a universal safe wind-speed threshold. Operational limits must be obtained from the relevant equipment documentation and approved site procedures.

## Temperature, Humidity, and Pressure

Environmental temperature, humidity, and atmospheric pressure can influence observing conditions and equipment operation.

High humidity may increase condensation risk under some conditions. Temperature changes can contribute to thermal gradients and affect equipment stability. Pressure readings can provide additional context for environmental monitoring.

These measurements should be interpreted together with the observatory's dew-point monitoring, equipment specifications, and local operating procedures when available.

## Observation Suitability Assessment

A basic assessment of observation suitability may consider:

- Cloud cover and transparency.
- Atmospheric seeing.
- Wind speed.
- Temperature and humidity.
- Telescope tracking performance.
- Camera status and image quality.
- Active alarms and site-specific restrictions.

No single metric should determine observation suitability unless an approved observatory policy explicitly defines that rule.

## Recommended Diagnostic Sequence

When weather conditions deteriorate:

1. Inspect the latest weather measurements and their recent trends.
2. Check whether the measurements are valid and sufficiently recent.
3. Compare the values with the observatory's documented operating limits.
4. Review telescope, dome, and camera status.
5. Determine whether the observation plan needs review by an authorized operator.
6. Record the measurements and the evidence supporting the assessment.

## Safety and Operational Boundaries

ASTRA may summarize environmental conditions and recommend that an operator review the observation plan. It must not claim that observing is safe solely from generic thresholds or override site-specific weather interlocks and safety procedures.
